Statistics · Julia · Public data

I build open-source tools that turn awkward public data into something you can analyse in a few lines — health records, elections, crime statistics, the central bank.

Most of my work lives in Julia: packages that read DATASUS microdata with constant memory, fetch TSE electoral results without unzipping a thing, and test election data honestly before anyone draws a conclusion. Between packages, I do reproducible research on the climate of the São Francisco valley.

About

003

I'm a statistician in Petrolina, in the semi-arid northeast of Brazil. I care about the unglamorous part of data work: getting the numbers out of the systems that publish them, in a shape that stays stable year after year.

Brazilian public data is rich and hard to reach — compressed DBC files, Latin-1 encodings, layouts that change between releases. My packages absorb that mess once, so the analysis can start at the question instead of at the file format.

I also like tools that are honest about their limits. If a classical test does not hold for electoral counts, the package should say so before you publish.

Reproducible paper · LaTeX + Julia · 2025

Less rain and more heat in Petrolina, Pernambuco (1961–2025)

How often does a year as dry as 2012/13 come back, and has the local climate shifted? Built on INMET's conventional station 82983, cross-checked against the automatic station A307, neighbouring stations, ANA rain gauges and the official climate normals, with a regional frequency analysis for return periods.

Read the paper & code ↗
  1. What is the return period of a hydrological year as dry as 2012/13, how does it change under non-stationarity, and do dry years persist?
  2. Has the regime changed in temperature, humidity, wind, climate extremes (ETCCDI) and the seasonality of rainfall?
